AI-GENERATED BIOGRAPHIES

Meet our new AI-generated biographies: a pilot exploring how carefully used AI can help visitors discover the people, companies and organisations behind the objects in our collection.

Our collection includes more than 25,000 people and organisations, but many of their records are sparse: a date, an occupation, perhaps a birthplace. The richer context often sits on the object pages they're linked to, leaving visitors to piece it together. Writing a curator-authored biography for every record would take many years, so this pilot tests whether AI, used carefully and transparently, can bring that knowledge together into a useful starting point.

What the biographies do

Each biography is shaped around the objects we hold. Rather than reproducing a general life story, it explains why a person or organisation appears in our collection and how they connect to the things we care for, giving visitors more context and more routes into the collection.

Biographies are clearly labelled as AI-generated and only appear where our existing catalogue entry is short. Where a curator has written a fuller biography, we show that instead.

How they're made

An AI model writes each biography using:

  • Our own object records and the biographical data we hold, such as dates, places and occupations.
  • Selected facts from Wikidata, a structured knowledge base.
  • Where available, Wikipedia. Because it is openly edited, it carries the least weight.

The model is instructed to take facts from these sources rather than its general training data. Where sources disagree, our own collection data takes priority.

Safeguards

AI-generated text can be wrong, so we've built in checks:

  • Link checking. Links to other objects and people are checked against our catalogue, and unverifiable links are removed before publication.
  • Minimum data. If there isn't enough information to write something meaningful, we don't publish a biography.
  • Curator review. Senior curators have reviewed a range of biographies during the pilot to improve their accuracy and tone.
  • Audit trail. For every biography we record the sources, prompt and model used, and when it was generated.
  • Reporting. Every biography has a “Report an issue with this summary” link so you can flag problems directly.

What this isn't

  • Not a replacement for curators. Curator-written records remain the primary source of scholarship on our collection. AI biographies fill gaps where we'd otherwise have almost nothing.
  • Not part of our core catalogue. The biographies aren't stored in our collection management system. They're a feature of this website that we review regularly and can switch off at any time.

We're testing this in public and listening to feedback, to learn where AI can responsibly improve access to museum collections, where it falls short, and what role it should or shouldn't play in future. By doing this carefully and openly, the Science Museum Group is helping put the museum sector at the forefront of the conversation about AI: not as a novelty, but as a practical, transparent way to make collections more useful, discoverable and connected.

Report an issue

Use the “Report an issue with this summary” link on any AI-generated biography to flag inaccuracies, tone or anything else that looks wrong.

For broader comments on the pilot, email web.team@sciencemuseum.ac.uk with “AI Biographies” in the subject line.
