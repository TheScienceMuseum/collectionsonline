AI-GENERATED BIOGRAPHIES

We are piloting new AI-generated biographies, exploring how carefully used AI can help the public discover the people, companies and organisations behind the objects in our collection.

Our online collection includes more than 25,000 people and organisations, but many of their records are sparse, with just a date, an occupation, or perhaps a birthplace. Richer context often sits on object pages that the individuals are connected to, which leaves the public to piece the stories together themselves.

Writing curator-authored biographies for every record would take many years, so this pilot tests whether AI, used carefully and transparently, can bring existing knowledge together to provide a useful starting point.

About the AI-generated biographies

Each biography is shaped around the objects we care for in the national collection. Rather than reproducing a general life story, the new biographies explain why a person or organisation appears in our collection and how they connect to the object, providing more context and more routes into the collection.

Biographies are clearly labelled as AI-generated and only appear where our existing catalogue entry is short. Where a curator has written a fuller biography, that is published instead.

How the biographies are made

An AI model writes each biography using:

  • Our existing object records and the biographical data we hold, such as dates, places and occupations.
  • Selected facts from Wikidata, a structured knowledge base.
  • Where available, Wikipedia. However as this is openly edited, this carries the least weight when creating the biography.

The AI model is instructed to take facts from these sources rather than its general training data. Where sources disagree, our own collection data takes priority.

Safeguards

AI-generated text can be wrong, so we've built in checks:

  • Link checking. Links to other objects and people are checked against our catalogue, and unverifiable links are removed before publication.
  • Minimum data. If there isn't enough information to write something meaningful, we don't publish a biography.
  • Curator review. Senior curators have reviewed a range of biographies during the pilot to improve their accuracy and tone.
  • Audit trail. For every biography we record the sources, prompt and model used, and when it was generated.
  • Reporting. Every biography has a “Report an issue with this summary” link so you can flag problems directly.

We're testing the AI-generated biographies in public and will be listening to feedback, working carefully and transparently to help make our collection more useful, discoverable and connected.

Report an issue

Use the “Report an issue with this summary” link on any AI-generated biography to flag inaccuracies, tone or anything else that looks wrong.

For broader comments on the pilot, email web.team@sciencemuseum.ac.uk with “AI Biographies” in the subject line.
