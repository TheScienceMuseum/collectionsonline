ABOUT AI-GENERATED BIOGRAPHIES

Meet our new AI-generated biographies: a pilot exploring how carefully controlled AI can help visitors discover the people, companies and organisations behind the objects in our collection.

Across Collection Online, thousands of records contain only the barest details about a person or organisation, a date, a role, or a single line. This pilot asks whether AI, used carefully and transparently, can bring together knowledge already held across our object records to give visitors and researchers a clearer starting point: who these people and organisations were, how they connect to the objects we care for, and why those connections matter.

These biographies are clearly labelled wherever they appear. They sit alongside short curator-written entries when we have enough underlying data to write something meaningful and are not generated or shown where a curator has already written a fuller biography.

This is a pilot. We are testing it in public, labelling the biographies clearly and listening to feedback so we can learn where AI can responsibly improve access to museum collections, where it falls short, and what role it should, or should not, play in future. By doing this carefully and openly, SMG is helping put the museum sector at the forefront of the discussion about AI, not as a novelty, but as a practical, transparent way to make collections more useful, discoverable and connected.

Why we're doing this

The SMG collection contains records for more than 25,000 people and organisations. Many are sparse, an occupation, a date, perhaps a birthplace, while much of the richer context we hold about them lives elsewhere on the object pages they are connected to. This means visitors often have to move between individual object records to understand why a person or company appears in the collection at all. Writing a curator-authored biography for every record would take many years of specialist work, so this pilot tests whether AI can bring existing collection knowledge together in a useful, transparent and proportionate way.

We think an AI-generated biography, grounded in our own data, especially in the information already held on existing records and supplemented by a small number of trusted third party sources such as Wikidata and Wikipedia, can give visitors a more useful starting point. It can explain why a person, company or organisation appears in Collection Online, show how they connect to the objects we hold, and give those objects more context than a sparse name-and-date record can provide.

For visitors, the benefit is hopefully more routes into the collection, more context for the objects they are looking at and a better chance of discovering connections they might otherwise miss.

Crucially, we are not simply reproducing biography text held elsewhere. Each biography is shaped around the objects we actually hold. It explains why a particular person, company or organisation matters in the context of our collection, how they connect to the things we care for. The aim is not to simply reproduce a formal biography on our website but to show the specific relationship between that person or organisation and the objects held by the Science Museum Group.

How the biographies are generated

Each biography is produced by an AI model drawing on:

  • Our own object records (the objects this person or organisation made, owned, or is linked to).
  • The structured biographical data we already hold (dates, places, occupation, nationality).
  • A select number of fields from Wikidata, a publicly editable knowledge base of structured, sourced facts, and Wikipedia. While we acknowledge that Wikipedia is susceptible to editorial concerns, we require that the model itself is also supportive of these claims, which helps act as a guardrail against malicious Wikipedia edits. Wikipedia itself also has extensive measures in place to deal with misinformation.
  • Priority is given to museum data in all cases.

The AI is instructed to draw its substantive biographical claims from those sources rather than from its general training data. Where it makes links to other objects in the collection, those links are checked against our catalogue before the biography is saved, and unverifiable links are removed. We keep a record of every biography: which data informed it, which prompt was used, which model produced it, and when it was generated.

Safeguards

AI-generated text can be wrong. We've built the system with this in mind:

  • Grounded in our data. Biographies are built from our own collection records plus additional data from Wikidata and Wikipedia. The model is instructed to rely on those sources for the substantive facts of a person's life and work, rather than drawing freely on its training data.
  • Link checking. Any link in the biography to a collection object or person is checked against our catalogue; unverifiable links are removed before publication.
  • Minimum data threshold. If the model indicates it doesn't have enough information to write a meaningful summary, nothing is published for that record.
  • Flagging. Every AI biography carries a “Report an issue with this summary” option, so visitors can flag a problem directly.
  • Transparency. Every biography is labelled as AI-generated, and we publish this page explaining what we're doing and why.
  • Curatorial oversight. Throughout the development of the pilot our senior curators have reviewed a range of generated biographies to help us improve the tone and accuracy of the responses it generates.
  • Audit trail. For every biography, we log the prompt and model version used to generate it, alongside the source data it drew on and the date it was produced. This gives us a full audit trail we can revisit if a summary is ever questioned.

What this isn't

  • Not a replacement for curatorial work. Curator-authored biography records remain the primary source of scholarship on our collection. AI summaries are a supplement for biography records that would otherwise have almost nothing.
  • Not part of our core collection management data. These biographies are not stored in our core collection management system. This is purely a Collections Online feature that we'll review on a rolling basis and can switch off at any point.
